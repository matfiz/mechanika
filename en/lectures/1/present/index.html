Lecture 1

States, determinism and phase portraits

This course asks one question: what is a classical mechanical system? This lecture gives the first, provisional answers. The state is the data at one instant that determine a system’s future. The states form the phase space, and the law of motion defines a flow on it. The flow exists for all time when the potential is C2C^{2}C2 on all of Rn\R^{n}Rn and bounded below. For one degree of freedom, such as the pendulum, the graph of the potential determines the phase portrait.

1.1 Determinacy

Newton’s principle

A system of NNN point particles in space is described, at each instant, by the positions r1,…,rN∈R3\mathbf r_1,\dots,\mathbf r_N\in\R^{3}r1​,…,rN​∈R3. Collect them into one list x=(r1,…,rN)∈Rn,n=3N. x=(\mathbf r_1,\dots,\mathbf r_N)\in\R^{n},\qquad n=3N . x=(r1​,…,rN​)∈Rn,n=3N.(1.1) The point xxx is the configuration, and Rn\R^{n}Rn is the configuration space. Two particles on a line define one point in the plane of configurations. A motion of the whole system is a single curve x(t)=(r1(t),…,rN(t)),x(t)=(\mathbf r_1(t),\dots,\mathbf r_N(t)) ,x(t)=(r1​(t),…,rN​(t)), where ttt is the time.

Newton’s second law says the accelerations are functions of the positions and velocities (and possibly the time), x¨=f(x,x˙,t). \ddot x=f(x,\dot x,t). x¨=f(x,x˙,t).(1.2) Its physical content is Newton’s principle of determinacy: positions and velocities at one instant determine the whole motion (Arnold, Mathematical Methods, §1). Definition 1.1 (State, phase space). A state of a system is a collection of data at one instant that determines its motion at every other instant at which the motion exists. The set of all states is the phase space M\phaseM. When fff in (1.2) does not depend on ttt (and solutions are unique, §1.2), a state is the pair z=(x,v)∈M⊆R2n,v=x˙. z=(x,v)\in\phase\subseteq\R^{2n},\qquad v=\dot x . z=(x,v)∈M⊆R2n,v=x˙.(1.3)

The equation x¨=t\ddot x=tx¨=t is an example. The time must then be included in the state, giving the (2n+1)(2n+1)(2n+1)-dimensional space of (1.6).

1.6 Why phase space, and a contrast

Why phase space

In configuration space, or in configuration space and time, curves cross: the motions of Figure 1.1 meet again at t=πt=\pit=π, and a pendulum passes the bottom going both ways. In phase space they cannot: by Corollary 1.6 through every state there is exactly one orbit. Thus determinacy makes a phase portrait a family of non-intersecting orbits, even when the trajectories in configuration space cross.

Question. What property of the flows of mechanics — as opposed to the damped pendulum — forbids attractors?

Energy conservation already rules out asymptotically stable equilibria: a nearby state of different energy can never reach the equilibrium’s energy. Energy conservation alone says less about other attractors, such as limit cycles. Area gives another clue. The frictionless flow deforms a region of initial states while preserving its area. The damped flow shrinks the area at a rate determined by the divergence of the vector field. The divergences are 000 and −γ-\gamma−γ, respectively. Lecture 7 explains this connection and answers the question.
