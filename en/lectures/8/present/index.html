Lecture 8

Canonical maps and generating functions

Hamilton’s equations survive a far larger freedom of coordinates than Lagrange’s: qqq and ppp may be mixed. This lecture asks how a map that keeps Hamilton’s equations is recognised, and how one is built. It is recognised by its brackets, and equivalently by one explicit 111-form being closed. On a star-shaped set a closed 111-form is the differential of a function, so a single function of 2n2n2n variables, a generating function, determines a canonical map where the required coordinates are independent. A generating function may also depend on time, and then the Hamiltonian changes by ∂F2/∂t\partial F_2/\partial t∂F2​/∂t. The lecture ends with the question the next one answers: which F2F_2F2​ makes the new Hamiltonian vanish?
