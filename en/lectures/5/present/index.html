Lecture 5

Observables and the Poisson algebra

Lecture 4 gave Hamilton’s equations. This lecture asks how an observable — anything one can measure on a state, a function on phase space — changes along a motion. The answer is its bracket with the Hamiltonian. The Poisson bracket makes the observables an algebra. We prove its properties, including the Jacobi identity, and express conservation laws as bracket equations. Conserved quantities then form a Lie algebra. Angular momentum gives the example so(3)\so(3)so(3). Finally, we describe time evolution by letting observables change while keeping the initial state fixed. These structures are the first part of the course’s second answer.

Proposition 5.16 (Degree counting). If fff and ggg are polynomials in (q,p)(q,p)(q,p) of degrees aaa and bbb, then {f,g}\pb fg{f,g} is a polynomial of degree at most a+b−2a+b-2a+b−2. If fff and ggg are homogeneous, {f,g}\pb fg{f,g} is homogeneous of degree a+b−2a+b-2a+b−2, or zero.
