Lecture 12

Integrability, and what a mechanical system is

Lecture 11 ended with the rigid body moving along the curves where a sphere meets an energy ellipsoid. The pendulum and the central force were solved the same way, by conserved quantities (Lectures 1 and 10). What do nnn conserved quantities force in nnn degrees of freedom? One condition beyond conservation is needed, involution, a condition on their brackets. A level set on which the nnn differentials are independent is then an nnn-dimensional Lagrangian surface, invariant under all nnn flows. Each compact connected component is a torus carrying action–angle variables. The course closes by comparing the three descriptions of a mechanical system and explaining which structures quantum mechanics retains.

12.1 Lagrangian subspaces

Lecture 6 put a bilinear form behind the bracket: ω(u,v)=uTJv\symp(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}\JJ vω(u,v)=uTJv on R2n\R^{2n}R2n (Definition 6.11; the matrix has been written J\JJJ since Lecture 10). For ω\sympω every line does, since antisymmetry gives ω(w,w)=0\symp(w,w)=0ω(w,w)=0.

Summary

  • Handed on to Integrability: integrals in involution, at most nnn of them independent at a point (Definition 12.7 and Proposition 12.6); for nnn functions in involution, a level set at each point of which the dFi\dd F_idFi​ are independent is Lagrangian and invariant under all nnn flows (Theorem 12.9); a compact connected component of it is a torus with actions Ik=12π∮γkp⋅dqI_k=\frac1{2\pi}\oint_{\gamma_k}p\cdot\dd qIk​=2π1​∮γk​​p⋅dq (Theorem 12.10, cited, whose proof is theirs).
  • The third answer: a classical mechanical system is a Poisson algebra of observables, a Hamiltonian and a convex set of states (Definition 7.10), described at three levels (§12.5).
  • Handed on to quantum mechanics: the algebra, the states, and observables that generate automorphisms, for which Jacobi is the condition ((12.4), Theorem 6.5); not the points, since [Q,P]=iℏ 1\comm QP=\ii\hbar\,\Id[Q,P]=iℏ1 leaves no character; the exact correspondence on hn⋊sp(2n)\mathfrak h_n\rtimes\splie(2n)hn​⋊sp(2n) (Proposition 12.13, (12.6)), which in one degree of freedom cannot be extended to all polynomials (Theorem 12.14).
