Lecture 4

The Legendre transform and Hamilton’s equations

Lecture 3 gave the first answer: a motion makes the action stationary, and its momentum is p=∂L/∂q˙p=\partial\Lag/\partial\dot qp=∂L/∂q˙​. So far a state has been a position with a velocity. This lecture asks when the velocity can be traded for the momentum. Momentum is a covector and transforms differently from velocity. The Legendre transform makes the change possible when the Lagrangian is hyperregular. Lagrange’s nnn second-order equations then become Hamilton’s 2n2n2n first-order equations on the space of positions and momenta, the phase space, with an action principle of their own. This takes us from the Lagrange description to the Hamilton description used in Part II.
