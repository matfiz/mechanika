Lecture 2

Constraints and Lagrange’s equations

Lecture 1 gave the state and the flow of a system whose particles move freely. Rods, hinges and rigid bodies constrain their motion. The allowed configurations form a surface in the space of particle positions. This lecture asks what Newton’s law becomes on such a surface. It explains when constraint equations define a surface, identifies the allowed velocities, and states the assumption on the constraint forces. For potential forces and fixed ideal constraints, Newton’s law is equivalent to Lagrange’s equations for L=T−V\Lag=T-VL=T−V in any coordinates on the surface. With the regularity specified below, (q,q˙)(q,\dot q)(q,q˙​) at a given time determines the motion. The pendulum illustrates the construction.

Summary

  • Handed on: the configuration space Q\configQ, a surface of dimension nnn covered by patches of generalised coordinates with smooth transitions (Corollary 2.4 and Lemma 2.5); its velocities TrQ=ker⁡DfT_r\config=\ker DfTr​Q=kerDf, which transform by DφD\varphiDφ (Theorem 2.7, (2.4)).
  • The main result: for fixed regular ideal constraints and potential forces, Newton’s law is equivalent to Lagrange’s equations for L=T−V\Lag=T-VL=T−V, in any patch (Theorem 2.10).
  • Determinacy: for C3C^{3}C3 data, (q,q˙)(q,\dot q)(q,q˙​) at a time t0t_0t0​ determines the motion for as long as it stays in the patch (Corollary 2.11).
