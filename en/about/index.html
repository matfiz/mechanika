About this site

The companion to Theoretical Mechanics — what a classical mechanical system is.

What is here

12 lectures with 44 definitions, 96 numbered results, 90 proofs, 15 worked examples and 35 figures. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

The course asks one question, what a classical mechanical system is, and answers it three times, in twelve lectures and four parts: as a Lagrangian, as a Poisson algebra with a Hamiltonian and a convex set of states, and with the three layers side by side. The exercise sheets, one per lecture, are drafted in English and will appear here lecture by lecture.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. Every formula, sign and constant the notes print is checked by a computer-algebra script before it is printed.

Questions and solutions

An exercise is a question, and a solution is the full worked method. This site is always the questions-only edition: worked solutions are done in the tutorial, which is where the learning in them actually happens.

They are not merely hidden here. Every solution is deleted from the source text before it reaches the converter, the public sheet PDFs are recompiled as the questions-only variant rather than copied from the repository, and the finished site and every PDF in it are searched for solution material before anything can be published.

Your privacy

There is no account or login. Your theme, checked learning goals and presentation annotations are saved in this browser. The progress page supports an optional Matfiz profile and a backup you can transfer to another device. Learning records are not sent to a server. Study prompts are editable; you choose whether to send one to an external assistant.

Found a mistake?

Please report it. Corrections during a course's first run are genuinely valuable — bring it to a lecture or tutorial and it will be fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
